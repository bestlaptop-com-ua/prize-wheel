## 1. CRITICAL - the capture after every landing is silently cancelled

Evidence: on the current build (from line 6347) captures alternate: 9 ran, 10 were cancelled, and
1 spin had no capture (section 3). Every cancel is `PULSEFIRST reject=2 hz=0.0` (reason 2 =
DEADLINE: not one STEP pulse within 150 ms) and is the first capture attempted after a landing
verdict - a controlled landing or a dare-recovery landing (SPIN#6's cancel follows SPIN#4's
recovery; SPIN#5 in between attempted no capture). Every capture that ran was the first after a
boot or after a cancelled one. The morning build (lines 1-160) has no such cancel: 15 captures in
a row ran, each right after a landing and hold. The one afternoon abandon of this kind (4919,
'capture arming interlock/deadline', right after the landing at 4913) is the same failure seen
through the old 150 ms lease. Tonight's lease widening to 400 ms (pw_capture_lease.h 10, comment
'150 ms expired on a loop stall') only changed which check reports it.

Cause: `landingVerdict()` (ino 2384) calls `stepper->forceStop()` unconditionally. It was added at
12:57 today (fix19/fix20, comment: no pulse generator activity in hold, ever) and is not in the
morning builds. In FastAccelStepper 1.2.7, forceStop() only sets a one-shot immediate-stop flag
(RampGenerator.h 80, FastAccelStepper.cpp 512-520). The flag is consumed when the ramp generator
plans its next command (RampGenerator.cpp 251-257), and the queue filler does nothing while the
generator is idle (FastAccelStepper.cpp 220-222). A new move()/moveTo() clears the flag
(RampGenerator.cpp 131-139); runForward(), which every capture uses, does not (FastAccelStepper.cpp
461, RampGenerator.cpp 32-47). At landingVerdict the stepper is already idle (LANDING_SETTLE is
entered only after `!isRunning()`, ino 3049; recovery waits for the same), so the flag waits for
the next capture's runForward() and kills it at the first planning step. That dead capture
consumes the flag, so the capture after it works. Dare recovery uses move(), so recoveries always
run - and set the flag again at their own landing. The unguarded forceStop at the recovery
timeout (ino 3168) does the same to the first capture after `r`.

Effect: about half of the spins coast uncontrolled and stop wherever friction takes them; on a
dare that means dare recovery (3). The spin closes as NO_REACHABLE_SAFE, which hides the cause.

Fix (one line): guard the call like the other call sites (`if (stepper->isRunning())`) or delete
it - the stepper is provably idle there, and SOFT_HOLD already force-stops a generator that is
still running. Guard ino 3168 the same way. Update the header comment (line 24 still says
forceStop exists only in the fault path).

## 2. HIGH - controlled landings overshoot the chosen target

Evidence: 25 of 26 controlled landings in today's log stopped past their target (SUMMARY `err`:
median +3.5 deg; worst +11.0 deg, not counting SPIN#20 whose final includes a recovery move). At
RAMP_STOP the stop ramp was already committed to end on average 2.4 deg beyond the target
(lead - 2 - remain, all 28 RAMP_STOP lines). 6 of the 9 controlled landings on the current build
were EDGE_SAFE; SPIN#17 (6461-6464) ended 8.1 deg past its target, in the next wedge. Morning
SPIN#20 (114-119) overshot 10.3 deg, 0.1 deg into the label-16 dare, and dare recovery then walked
it 30 deg - one of the recover-after-stop events.

Cause: prepareCapturePlan (ino 1962-1976) sizes the brake with zero margin: the stopping distance
at the entry speed equals the distance remaining at planning time (pwPlanBrake). The first control
tick comes 25 ms after torque-on; by then the wheel has moved another 1-4 deg, the stepper's
stopping distance exceeds what is left, and the stop gate (serviceDecelTick, 2245-2255) fires on
that first tick and commits a ramp that ends beyond the target. STOP_GATE_EXTRA_DEG (meant to stop
2 deg early) never gets a chance. The phase snap at torque-on (4b) adds up to +/-3.6 deg.
Side effect: because the gate always fires on the first tick, the closed-loop braking code after
it (2257-2323: position profile, wheel tracking, resync) never runs. Every brake is one open-loop
stopMove ramp.

Effect: the 8 deg dare-edge margin is effectively 2-5 deg when the dare is ahead of the target.

Fix: plan the brake for (remaining - travel expected before the first tick - STOP_GATE_EXTRA_DEG),
so the gate fires with margin, or land on an absolute step position (moveTo). Never ask
FastAccelStepper for a position closer than its stopping distance (it would overshoot and reverse).

## 3. HIGH - dare recovery is the recover-after-stop, and it locks out the next spin

Evidence: 12 DARE_RECOVERY events today. 9 followed free coasts: 1 after a capture cancelled by
section 1 (6376), 3 after captures abandoned for other reasons (two launch-speed prerequisite
failures, fixed tonight, and one plan refusal), 5 in spins that attempted no capture. 1 followed a
controlled overshoot (117). 2 were second attempts after the first recovery stopped on or within
2 deg of the dare. 2488-2491: commanded +20.1 deg, the wheel went -10.7 deg, then a second move of
-29 deg (the driver dropped off SPI right after, see 5). 5188: recovery timed out after 8 s ->
LANDING_UNSAFE latched.

Leftover: startDareRecovery (ino 1701-1740, rationale in 339-343) was added 2026-09-21 for the
unbalanced wheel (coasts balance on the crest at label 8 or roll into the rest cone at 16). The
wheel was counterweighted at 10:10 today and the gravity model cleared, so the premise is gone; the
downhill choice now degenerates to the last spin direction. It still energizes 2200 mA from
freewheel (the rotor snaps up to +/-3.6 deg, see 4b) and walks the wheel to the neighbour wedge
centre at 0.07 rev/s (10-40 deg, 1-2 s), up to twice, with no guest or fight monitoring during the
powered move.

Side effect with persistent hold and the one-attempt-per-rest capture budget (pw_capture_cycle.h):
a recovery landing after an uncontrolled coast is not a qualifying landing (never energized), and
the hold keeps the wheel from resting in freewheel, so the next spin from the hold gets no capture
at all (SPIN#3 at 6290 and SPIN#16 at 2541: fast spins, no RESERVE). Together with section 1, one
recovery leaves the next two spins uncontrolled (6376 -> SPIN#5 no capture, SPIN#6 cancelled; the
same at 6287 -> 6290, 6300).

Fix: disable dare recovery (or limit it to label 8, one slow move) now that the wheel is balanced;
with 1 and 2 fixed it should be rare. Drop persistent hold (its premise, the unbalanced wheel
rolling off, is also gone) or let a released hold re-qualify the capture budget.

## 4. HIGH - why stops look abrupt

a) Pass 3 brakes to the nearest safe wedge. chooseSafeTarget pass 3 (ino 1282-1314) keeps the
smallest distance, i.e. the first safe interior past the minimum braking distance, which maximises
braking (cap 600 sps2). Log 34-57: q=1 captures planned a=355-408 sps2 with runways of 72-80 deg
against natural stops of 255-278 deg - stopping in 27-30% of the natural distance, about 3x the
natural deceleration - versus a=125-156 sps2 for the q=0 captures that morning. Pass 3 runs when
pass 1's window is closed (below roughly 0.25 rev/s at capture) and the natural stop is in or
within 5 deg of a dare, about a third of the wheel. It still happens on the current build (SPIN#10
at 6412, q=1; that capture was cancelled by section 1).
Fix: pick the farthest reachable safe interior (just short of the natural stop), the one that needs
the least braking.

b) Capture snap. Every TORQUE_ON logs phase_unverified=1: the TMC5160 microstep phase is arbitrary
relative to the rotor when EN goes low, so the rotor snaps to the nearest equilibrium of the field,
up to +/-2 full steps (+/-3.6 deg, about 29 mm at the rim), and the wheel is pulled after it. On
top of that, strong spins are captured at 0.39-0.40 rev/s while the entry command is clamped at
CAPTURE_MAX_CMD_REV_S 0.38 (ino 234), a 4-5% instant slowdown. That undoes the 2026-09-21 change of
TRAIL_FRACTION from 0.95 to 0.98 (ino 230: '5% field lag snapped the wheel at torque-on') for every
capture near the ceiling. The summaries' maxDecel of up to 3.6 rev/s2 is tens of times the planned
ramp (0.04-0.13 rev/s2); no 1 kHz trace of a capture exists yet to show where in the brake it
occurs.
The wheel is a large inertia on the motor's magnetic spring and swings at about 1 Hz, underdamped.
lastdump.txt (a recovery move at 2200 mA) shows it: the wheel first lags, then runs 27% faster
than the field (0.088 vs 0.069 rev/s), and it keeps swinging for about half a second after the
field has stopped. The same spring shows as speed hunting during braking: SUMMARY rise up to
0.065 rev/s tonight; the afternoon hunting (0.05-0.1 rev/s) tripped SUSTAINED_SPEEDUP on this
build at 4942 and 5069 until tonight's widening (ino 245 comment: rotor hunting tripped it).
Fix: let the entry command cover the launch tolerance (CAPTURE_MAX_CMD_REV_S >= 0.44 = 0.98 x
0.45) or capture at <= 0.38. Medium term: phase-aligned capture (calibrate the AS5600 angle against
the TMC5160 MSCNT once, then pre-step the driver so its phase matches the rotor before EN). That
needs the encoder INL corrected first: 1 deg of encoder error is 50 electrical degrees.

c) Constant-deceleration ramp and rigid stop. The ramp holds one deceleration all the way to zero,
about 1.5x what the wheel's own friction gives near the end of a coast, and LANDING_SETTLE then
holds 2200 mA, so the wheel stops a little harder than a free coast and cannot settle against the
flapper as a free wheel does. Lower priority.

## 5. CRITICAL for the party - latched faults silently switch steering off

Every enterFault() is persisted (PW_S1_PERSIST, ino 1614) and survives reboots; only `r` clears
it (plus `p` for DIR_CAL and tonight's auto-clear for SUSTAINED_SPEEDUP). While latched, spins
are observed but not steered (FAULT ACTIVE). Today: TMC_UART at 2493 -> 9 uncontrolled spins
including labels 3 and 8; SUSTAINED_SPEEDUP at 5069 -> 28 uncontrolled spins across a reboot (the
latch survives it), including label 16 six times and label 8 twice, until `r` at 5173.

Causes seen today:
- Driver drops off SPI or resets: DRV_STATUS, GSTAT and IOIN all ones at 2499 and 2504 (the fault
  itself was raised in SOFT_HOLD, 2493) and after a reboot at 6260-6270 (no answer until 24 V came
  up, 6272-6274); the 9/21 audit saw GSTAT=05 (reset + charge-pump undervoltage, i.e. VM low).
  The capture-time auto re-apply added tonight (ino 1950) only runs when GSTAT reads exactly 0x01
  with EN high. In IDLE the S2 check (every 5 s, pw_party_impl.h 447-469) and in SOFT_HOLD the
  health check (every 100 ms, ino 3123, EN low) latch TMC_UART instead. otpw (bit 26) is also
  treated as fatal (mask 0x1E000000, ino 1956).
- Boot before 24 V: setup() configures an unpowered driver and checkTmcUartOrFault() (ino 2738)
  latches TMC_UART, which then persists until `r`.
- SUSTAINED_SPEEDUP: 4 trips today (4942 and 5069 on this build, 4989 and 5019 on the certified_v2
  test), from speed hunting (4b). Widened tonight to 0.15 rev/s / 800 ms and auto-cleared after
  3 s at rest - the only fault that clears itself.
- LANDING_UNSAFE from a recovery timeout (5188).

Fix: auto-recover a driver reset at rest (GSTAT reset or uv_cp, or no answer): freewheel, wait for
VM, re-apply config, verify, clear - in IDLE and in hold. Do not persist a boot-time driver-absent
fault. Treat otpw as a warning (lower the current). Hardware: check the 24 V supply and its wiring
and add a bulk capacitor at the driver's VM input.

## 6. The guest re-spin that nobody did

The only GUEST-RESPIN in today's log (5013) came from the certified_v2 test build
(work/certified_v2_20260922: the August party-certified state machine ported to the new hardware;
it ran around lines 4946-5022 and logs `SPIN#n CAPTURE fasDir=`). It engaged at 0.742 rev/s with
the field 8% slower (cmd0 0.680). GUEST-RESPIN needs the measured speed above max(0.80, cmd + 0.25)
= 0.93 rev/s for 60 ms. The field was slower than the wheel and never speeds up after capture, so
the motor could only brake the wheel; raising a wheel of this size by 0.19 rev/s would take about
half a second of full motor torque in the forward direction. What the encoder saw was most likely
the rotor bouncing between the field and the wheel - the encoder sits on the motor shaft, and the
jaw coupling's elastomer spider is a spring. The same build tripped SUSTAINED_SPEEDUP on the
capture before (4989) and after (5019). The August build used the same detector with the same
engagement speeds (up to 0.72 rev/s), so the August phantom re-spins were most likely the same kind
of engagement transient.

Latent bug in the current code: controlSafetyChecks runs controlSpeedSafe (instant, > 0.80 rev/s,
ino 2107-2113) before the guest-override check (> max(0.80, cmd + 0.25) for 60 ms, 2138-2151).
LANDING_SETTLE does the same (3059, then 3067). The guest-respin path is therefore unreachable: a
real guest re-spin during braking or settling latches CONTROL_OVERSPEED (persisted, no auto-clear)
and steering stays off until `r`. The fault message still says 0.30 rps.

The guest sound is a separate path: the party build plays the guest jingle only for GUEST_STOPPED
(pw_party_impl.h 229-233), which also covers a coast in which contactDetected() (ino 2791-2805)
saw more than 1 rev/s2 of deceleration for 100 ms. Nothing in today's log shows it misfiring, but
it is the path to watch if the jingle plays when nobody touched the wheel.

Fix: check the guest override before the overspeed fault (release, close as GUEST_RESPUN, no
latch), debounce the overspeed fault, and make both require the rise to outlast a capture
transient.

## 7. Frame and encoder integrity

- rawZero moved from 2376 (set 9/21 17:10) to 1947 tonight (6340): some time today the frame moved
  by 38 deg - nearly two wedges - and nothing in the firmware noticed; it was found by eye (NOTES).
  The AS5600 sits on the motor rear stub, so slip anywhere between motor shaft and wheel (adapter,
  coupling, hub) or of the magnet on its stub is invisible to the firmware. Find the slip (paint
  marks across shaft, coupling, hub and magnet; check after a session). Consider a wheel-side index
  (hall sensor on the frame, magnet on the wheel) that checks the frame every revolution.
- The AS5600 INL correction has never been active. FINDINGS measured 3.8 deg peak-to-peak (+/-5%
  false speed ripple) before the magnet was re-seated on 9/21; the model stored at 17:10 that day
  had INL zeros, and today's G0,0,0,0,0,0 cleared gravity and INL together. The re-seated mount has
  not been measured. If it is similar, the error is as large as the 2 deg dare-proximity guard, and
  it blocks phase-aligned capture (4b).
- The online friction fit is noise on this wheel: 17 FRICTION_REJECT against 6 accepted fits today;
  rejected fits had c from -1.7 to +1.9, and accepted ones disagree by more than 3x (c 0.18-0.65).
  Natural-stop predictions float between builds and seeds (0.17/0.05, 0.25/0.05 and 0.55/0.28 all
  appear in today's log).

## 8. Serial prints stall the control loop

The loop-budget tracker shows loop max 23430 us (6315) and 34341 us (5159). Serial is UART0 at
115200, and HardwareSerial has no TX buffer by default, so a print blocks until all but the last
128 bytes (the hardware FIFO) are sent, about 87 us per byte: a SPIN SUMMARY (~400 bytes) blocks
~24 ms - that is the 23 ms maximum; an `s` status ~7 ms; a FRICTION line plus the RESERVE line in
one pass ~5 ms, right when the capture is being set up; the `d` diagnostic dump (~2300 lines)
about 12 s, during which the wheel is not watched at all. The SUMMARY prints happen at rest, so
today they did little harm, but any gap over 20 ms makes the encoder treat the interval as blind
(re-prime, velocity invalid). The lease comment ('150 ms expired on a loop stall') blamed this;
the evidence points to section 1 instead.
Fix: `::Serial.setTxBufferSize(4096)` before `begin` in PwMirrorSerial::begin (pw_party.h 135), or
a faster baud (update monitor.py too).

  test plays.

## 11. Suggested order (small, testable steps; one flash each)

1. Guard the landing forceStop and the recovery-timeout one (1). Expect: no more `reject=2 hz=0.0`.
2. Brake plan with margin; entry command covers the launch tolerance (2, 4b). Expect: `err` near 0,
   fewer EDGE_SAFE.
3. Pass 3 picks the farthest safe interior (4a). Expect: no plans above about 200 sps2.
4. Disable dare recovery; drop persistent hold or re-qualify the capture budget on hold release (3).
5. Fault policy: guest override before a debounced overspeed; auto-recover driver resets at rest;
   no persisted boot-time driver fault (5, 6).
6. Serial TX buffer (8).
7. Find the 38 deg slip; INL calibration; freeze friction at measured values (7).
